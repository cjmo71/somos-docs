Sound presets let you lock in exactly how a room should sound for a given situation — movie night, background music, late-night listening — and bring it all back instantly with a single tap. Every room has three music presets on its music page; a soundbar room has three TV presets on its room page as well.

## Where the presets live

- **Room page (soundbar rooms)** — the **Sound Presets** row sits between the volume card and the EQ card. These three slots are the room's _TV_ presets and carry a small TV icon once saved. On a soundbar, while **Music** is lit, the room page keeps only its header and volume — the presets you see then are the music ones, on the music page. Tap **TV** and the TV presets are back. A room without a soundbar — a single speaker, a stereo pair, an Amp — has no TV presets; its presets are on the music page.
- **Music page** — tap **Music** in the source row at the top of a room to open it. It has its own **Sound Presets** row with three _music_ slots (music-note icon), which can also capture the Music Level and Ambient/Full setting that only exist on that page.
- **Lock Screen** — a TV room's three presets are also chips on the [Live Activity](/features/live-activity) card, by name, with the loaded one lit. Tap one there and the room page follows.
- **Apple Watch** — the [Watch app](/features/apple-watch) shows the room's three presets under its volume: the TV presets while the room is on its TV input, the music presets otherwise. Tap one on the wrist and it lights on the phone and the Lock Screen too.

All six start out named **Preset 1**, **Preset 2** and **Preset 3**. A preset is stored per room — saving "Movies" in the Living Room does not change the Bedroom's presets. Signed in to iCloud, every preset follows your Apple ID to your other devices and comes back after a reinstall. See [iCloud Sync](/features/icloud-sync). On each preset button the TV or music icon sits in the top corner and the name sits below it, so a long name that wraps onto two lines never runs over the icon.

## What a preset captures

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Always saved">
    The sound itself is always captured — you cannot leave these out:

    - **Bass** and **Treble**
    - **Surround** level (rooms with surrounds)
    - **Height** level (height-capable soundbars and Era 300 satellites)
    - **Subwoofer** level (rooms with a Sub)
    - **Crossover** (rooms with a Sub whose speaker reports one)
    - On the Music sheet only: **Music Level** and **Ambient / Full** playback mode
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Only if you tick them">
    The save sheet has an **Also save** list. Anything left unticked is simply not part of the preset, and loading it leaves that setting exactly as the room has it now:

    - **Volume**
    - **Night Sound** (soundbar rooms, room page only)
    - **Speech Boost** (soundbar rooms, room page only)
    - **Loudness**

    An **All** row at the bottom ticks or clears every option offered for that room. The list only shows what the room can actually use — a music preset never offers Night Sound or Speech Boost, and a room without Loudness does not offer Loudness.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Saving, loading, and renaming presets

<Steps>
  <Step title="Dial in your sound">
    Adjust the EQ sliders and toggles until the room sounds exactly the way you want. If you want the preset to set the volume too, set that as well.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Hold a preset to save">
    Press and hold any preset button for about half a second. The **Save Preset** page opens with the name field (up to 14 characters) and the **Also save** tick list. Tick what you want the preset to carry, then tap **Save**. The button highlights as the active preset and a message confirms "_name_ saved". On a slot that has never been saved, typing a name and tapping the button is always a full save — the sound you dialled in is captured along with the name, first time.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Tap a preset to load it">
    Tap any card to apply everything it captured at once. The sliders move to the saved values and the speaker updates immediately. If the preset captured a volume, the room's volume changes too — so keep that in mind before ticking Volume on a preset you use late at night.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Rename without re-saving">
    Hold the button again on a preset you have already saved, change only the name and leave the ticks alone — the button reads **Rename** instead of **Save**, and every stored value is kept exactly as it was. Change any tick and it becomes a full **Save** again, re-capturing the room's current settings.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The note under the tick list says what is always saved: **Bass, treble and the levels are always saved**, or **Bass, treble, height and the levels** on a room with height.

## Deleting a preset

Hold a saved preset to open **Save Preset**, then tap **Delete preset** at the bottom. The slot is cleared and gets its first name back (**Preset 1**, **Preset 2** or **Preset 3**). Your speaker's sound doesn't change. **Delete preset** only shows on a preset that has been saved or named.

<Note>
  The tick list opens pre-filled with what the preset already holds, so a rename never silently drops Volume, Night Sound, Speech Boost or Loudness. Unticking something is a deliberate choice and clears it on Save.
</Note>

<Note>
  **If a preset says "loaded, but crossover didn't take".** Some speakers report a crossover but refuse to change it — an Arc with a Sub is the usual case. The first time a preset's crossover is refused, you see that message once; after that, presets in that room stop sending the crossover and load quietly. The crossover slider on the room page is not affected: move it yourself and, if the speaker accepts it, presets send the crossover again.
</Note>

## The highlight stays until you change the sound

The preset you loaded stays highlighted when you leave the room and come back, switch rooms, or close and reopen the app — the room remembers which one it is on, whether you loaded it on the page, from the widget, from the Lock Screen or from the Apple Watch. It clears in three cases:

- you move a slider the preset captured, or flip Night Sound, Speech Boost or Loudness on a preset that captured them — a toast says "_name_ preset was disabled", and your change stays in effect;
- a fresh read from the speaker no longer matches the preset's sound values — for example after a change in the official Sonos® app;
- you load another preset, which takes the highlight with it.

To bring a preset back, tap it again.

## Presets in a group

While rooms are grouped, they all use the presets of the room that leads the group. Every grouped room's page, room page and music page alike, shows the leader's presets under a heading with its name, for example **Office presets**. Loading or saving one acts on the leader. The **Group Rooms** sheet says so under the leader's name: **Presets come from Office**. When the rooms ungroup, each one has its own presets again, exactly as they were. See [Grouping Rooms](/rooms/groups).

## Presets in music-only rooms

Presets work in every room, not just soundbar rooms. A room without a soundbar — a single speaker, a stereo pair, an Amp, with or without a Sub — has its three presets on the music page, next to the EQ they capture; there are no TV presets on its room page. Night Sound and Speech Boost are never offered there, so the presets capture the EQ levels plus, optionally, Volume and Loudness.

## Presets from Siri and the widget

Once you name a preset, Siri knows it by that name — say "**Apply Movies in Somos InControl**" and it loads without any Shortcut setup. The Home Screen widget can also apply presets with one tap. See [Widget & Siri](/features/widget-and-siri).

<Tip>
  A good starting set for a soundbar room: **Movies** (Speech Boost on, a little extra bass), **Late Night** (Night Sound on, Volume ticked at a low level), and **Music** saved from the music page with Loudness on. Name them — the names are what you see on the Lock Screen card.
</Tip>
