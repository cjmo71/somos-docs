When Somos InControl detects a room built around a soundbar (Arc, Arc Ultra, Beam, Beam Ultra, Ray, Playbar, Playbase, or a newer bar with a TV input), it unlocks a set of home theater controls that only make sense in that context. They appear automatically in soundbar rooms and are hidden everywhere else, so your music-only rooms stay uncluttered. Only the soundbar decides this: a stereo pair, a single speaker or an Amp with a Sub bonded to it is still a music room, and never gets the TV controls or the TV presets.

## What every room gets

All rooms — single speakers, stereo pairs, portables and soundbar setups — share the same foundation:

- Volume slider with − / + and mute
- Bass and treble EQ, Loudness
- Subwoofer level, on/off, phase, and crossover (when a Sub is bonded and the speaker reports one)
- Three music presets on the music page
- Trueplay, Status Light and Touch Controls tiles
- The music page with your music services, favorites and the queue
- A room diagram, room grouping, and room rename

In a room without a soundbar, the bass, treble, Sub level and Loudness are on the [music page](/features/music) rather than the room page — see [TV rooms and music rooms](/rooms/overview#tv-rooms-and-music-rooms).

## Home-theater-only controls

  <Card title="TV / Music source and the title-bar button" icon="tv">
    At the top of a soundbar's page, the source row shows what the bar is doing — **TV** with its connection, or the music. The **Go to Music** button in the title bar opens the music page, and **Go to TV** brings you back. They change the page you are looking at, never the sound. While the bar plays music, a **Switch to TV** button on the now-playing line sends the soundbar back to its TV input — the same as pressing the input button on a remote. The source follows what the speaker is really doing, so music started elsewhere shows as music too.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Audio-format badge" icon="badge-check">
    Next to the source you'll see the connection (HDMI ARC, eARC, Optical) and the format the soundbar is decoding right now — Dolby Atmos, Dolby Digital 5.1, DTS, PCM Stereo and so on. It updates in real time, so you can confirm your TV is actually sending what you expect. While **Music** is lit, the same spot scrolls the format the music service reports for the track (**Lossless**, **Hi-Res Lossless**, **Dolby Atmos**) when there is one.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Night Sound" icon="moon">
    Compresses the loudest moments — explosions, music peaks — so you can follow the action without waking anyone. Save it into a preset and it is one tap away on the [Live Activity](/features/live-activity) too.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Speech Boost" icon="comment">
    Lifts dialogue above effects and music. On most soundbars it's an on/off tile; on the **Arc Ultra** it's a five-step slider (Off, Low, Medium, High, Max — Medium is roughly the old "on"). If the speaker refuses the change, the switch goes back and the app tells you instead of pretending it worked.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Surround level and on/off" icon="speaker">
    When surrounds are bonded: a **Surround** slider (−15 to +15) sets how loud the rears play for TV, and a **Surrounds** switch mutes them without unbonding. Music has its own **Music Level** slider and **Surround Music** tile (Ambient / Full) on the music page.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Height channel" icon="arrow-up">
    Sets the level of the overhead (Atmos) channels (−10 to +10). Shown only where there is real height hardware — the Arc family's up-firing drivers, the Beam (Gen 2), or Era 300 satellites — never on a Ray or Playbar.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Subwoofer" icon="volume-high">
    Level (−15 to +15), an on/off switch, **Sub phase** (Normal / Reversed — greyed out while Trueplay is on) and — where the speaker reports one — the crossover frequency (50–110 Hz). A second Sub, including two older-generation Subs, is supported. See [Sub phase](/features/eq-and-trueplay#sub-phase).
  </Card>
  <Card title="Group Audio Delay" icon="clock">
    Appears only while the soundbar is grouped with other rooms. Steps the delay applied to the other rooms so they don't echo against the bar — the same setting as the Sonos® app's Group Audio Delay.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Music speakers grouped with a soundbar

When a speaker that can't play TV on its own, such as an Era or a One, is in a group the soundbar leads, its page shows the soundbar's TV input: the **TV** row with the connection and audio format, and the **TV** and **Music** buttons. Those buttons act on the soundbar. Your TV remote still changes only the soundbar's volume; use the **Group** slider to move every room together. See [Grouping Rooms](/rooms/groups).

## Two pages for a soundbar room

A soundbar room has two pages. The room opens on what is playing: the **Home Theater** page for TV, the **Music** page for music. The **Go to TV** / **Go to Music** button in the title bar switches between them. The music page has its own presets and EQ; the EQ and presets page keeps a mini player, so you can still control the music from there. Switching pages does not change the sound.

{/* screenshot: IP-01, IP-02 (both pages of a soundbar room) */}

The sound you hear while music plays is music, so music's presets and EQ live on the [music page](/features/music). A room without a soundbar works this way all the time: its presets and EQ are always on the music page, and its room page keeps volume, Balance, the Sub's switch, crossover and phase, and the three switches.

## Building the home theater

Press and hold the room diagram on a soundbar's page to open **Home Theater Setup**, where you assign speakers to Front Left, Front Right, Surround Left, Surround Right, Subwoofer and Subwoofer 2 — including separate front speakers, which the official app doesn't offer. On an Arc Ultra or Beam Ultra, the surround slots offer only Era 100, Era 300, One, One SL and Five. See [Speaker Setup](/rooms/speaker-setup) for the full walkthrough.

## Subwoofer in non-soundbar rooms

<Note>
  The subwoofer controls are not exclusive to home theater rooms. They appear in **any** room that has a Sub bonded — including stereo pairs and single-speaker rooms: the Sub level on the music page, and the on/off switch, crossover and Sub phase on the room page. Bonding a Sub does not turn the room into a TV room. A Sub that isn't bonded to anything gets its own page explaining that there's nothing to control until you bond it to a soundbar or stereo pair.
</Note>
